Claims Examiner

Review pended claims, resolve NCCI/MUE edit failures, adjust allowed amounts, and finalize adjudication decisions from a single workbench. The Claims Examiner extends the auto-adjudication pipeline with a human-in-the-loop UI for claims that require manual judgment.

What you'll learn

How to open the examiner workbench, triage the pended-claims queue, inspect a claim and its adjudication trace, resolve edit failures, adjust line-level allowed amounts, and finalize a pay, deny, or pend-for-information decision — with a full audit trail.

Overview

Cloud Health Office auto-adjudicates 837P and 837I claims through the 10-step adjudication pipeline (code validation, coverage, provider, prior auth, benefits, NCCI/MUE, allowed amount, cost-sharing, and status update). Most claims finalize without human review. A subset — typically 5–15% depending on plan configuration — lands in a pended state and needs an examiner before it can pay or deny.

The Claims Examiner is the workbench for those claims. It exposes the same services the pipeline uses (claims-service, benefit-plan-service, payment-service), layered behind a UI that surfaces why the claim pended, what the pipeline already computed, and what actions are available.

FeatureDescription
Pended QueueFilter and sort pended claims by pend reason, age, payer, provider, or dollar amount.
Claim InspectorHeader, service lines, diagnosis codes, eligibility snapshot, and the adjudication trace from every pipeline step.
Edit ResolutionOverride, bypass, or accept NCCI / MUE edit failures with a required reason code.
Line AdjustmentsModify allowed amounts, units, or modifiers on individual service lines before finalization.
DecisionPay, deny, or pend-for-information (RFAI) with standard CARC / RARC reason codes.
Audit TrailEvery examiner action is recorded with user, timestamp, before/after values, and reason.

Who Should Use It

The Claims Examiner is intended for users whose role includes manual claim adjudication:

  • Claims examiners — day-to-day review and finalization of pended claims.
  • Claims leads / supervisors — queue assignment, override approvals, and audit review.
  • Medical directors — clinical review of claims pended for medical necessity.
  • Configuration analysts — to confirm whether a pend pattern is caused by benefit plan or fee schedule misconfiguration.
Role required

Access is gated by the claims.examiner role. Supervisors additionally need claims.examiner.supervisor to approve high-dollar overrides. Roles are assigned in the tenant's identity provider and enforced server-side — users without the role will not see the Claims Examiner menu item.

1. Open the Workbench

1

Sign in and open Claims Examiner

From the Demo Portal or your deployed instance, sign in and select Claims → Examiner Workbench from the main navigation.

The workbench loads with the pended-claims queue scoped to your tenant. If you belong to more than one tenant, use the tenant switcher in the top-right to pick the one you want to work in.

Tenant isolation

The examiner enforces the same X-Tenant-ID isolation as every other Cloud Health Office service. You will never see claims belonging to another tenant, even with the supervisor role.

2. Triage the Queue

2

Filter, sort, and claim work

The queue shows one row per pended claim with these columns:

ColumnMeaning
Claim #Payer claim number (maps to 837 CLM01).
MemberMember ID and name.
ProviderBilling NPI and name.
Pend ReasonPrimary reason the pipeline pended the claim (e.g. NCCI_CONFLICT, NO_FEE_SCHEDULE, ELIGIBILITY_GAP, MANUAL_REVIEW).
AgeCalendar days since receipt — drives regulatory timeliness SLAs.
BilledTotal billed amount on the claim.
AssigneeExaminer the claim is currently checked out to (blank if unassigned).

Use the filter bar at the top of the queue to narrow by pend reason, age bucket, payer, provider, or dollar range. Click any column header to sort. Click Claim on a row to open it in the inspector — this also checks the claim out to you so another examiner does not pick it up at the same time.

3. Inspect a Claim

3

Review header, lines, and adjudication trace

The inspector has three panels:

  • Header — member, subscriber, provider, payer, service dates, diagnosis codes, prior auth references.
  • Service lines — CPT/HCPCS, modifiers, units, POS, billed, and the currently-computed allowed, plan paid, and member responsibility.
  • Adjudication trace — step-by-step output from the 10-step pipeline, including which NCCI/MUE edits fired, which fee schedule was looked up, and how cost-sharing was computed.

Use the trace panel to understand why the claim pended before you change anything. The trace is read-only and immutable — it records the state the pipeline produced when it pended the claim.

4. Resolve Edit Failures

4

Accept, override, or bypass NCCI / MUE edits

If the pend reason is an edit failure, open the Edits tab on the inspector. Each failing edit shows the CPT pair, the NCCI/MUE indicator, and the modifier rule that would allow an override.

For each edit you have three actions:

  • Accept edit — the edit is correct; the denied line stays denied. Pick a CARC / RARC reason code.
  • Override — bypass the edit and allow the line. Requires a documented override reason and, above the configured dollar threshold, a supervisor approval.
  • Add modifier — attach a modifier (e.g. 59, XE, XS) that legitimately resolves the NCCI conflict per CMS guidance.
Overrides are audited

Every override is recorded with your user ID, timestamp, reason code, and free-text justification. Plan administrators receive a weekly override report for compliance review.

5. Adjust Allowed Amounts

5

Modify line-level allowed amounts

On the Lines tab, each service line has an editable Allowed field. Typical reasons to adjust:

  • Fee schedule lookup returned no match and the pipeline pended to let an examiner price the line manually.
  • Negotiated single-case agreement pricing that is not yet loaded into the fee schedule.
  • Out-of-network pricing adjustment based on a Qualified Payment Amount (QPA).

When you change an allowed amount, the inspector re-runs benefit calculation and cost-sharing against the current plan and shows you the new plan-paid and member-responsibility values before you commit. Use the Preview button to compare the old and new totals side-by-side.

6. Finalize the Decision

6

Pay, deny, or pend for information

When the claim is ready, click Finalize in the inspector toolbar and pick one of:

DecisionResult
PayClaim moves to the next payment batch. A downstream 835 ERA will reflect the final allowed, plan-paid, and member-responsibility amounts.
DenyClaim is denied with the CARC/RARC codes you picked. A denial 835 is generated on the next batch cycle.
Pend for information (RFAI)Generates a 277 Request for Additional Information to the provider. The claim re-enters the examiner queue when the response arrives.

Finalization is transactional: the claim status update, audit entry, and payment queueing all commit together. If any step fails the claim stays checked out to you and you can retry.

Timeliness

The workbench surfaces the regulatory SLA clock next to every claim (e.g. 30 days for clean claims under most state prompt-pay laws). Pended claims approaching their deadline are highlighted in amber; past-deadline claims in red.

Audit Trail

Every action taken in the Claims Examiner is persisted to an immutable audit log — queue check-out, edit override, allowed-amount adjustment, decision finalization, and supervisor approval. The log captures:

  • User ID and display name
  • UTC timestamp
  • Action type and target (claim, line, edit)
  • Before and after values
  • Reason code and free-text justification
  • Supervisor approval reference (where required)

Supervisors can open the audit trail on any claim from the inspector's History tab. The same records are exported nightly to the tenant's compliance bucket for long-term retention and are available via the Claims Audit API for integration with GRC tooling.

Troubleshooting

SymptomLikely cause & fix
Examiner Workbench menu item is missing after sign-in Your account does not have the claims.examiner role. Ask a tenant administrator to assign it in your identity provider.
Queue is empty but you expect pended claims Confirm the tenant switcher is set to the correct tenant, then clear any active queue filters. If the queue is still empty, the pipeline may have auto-finalized the claims — check the claims search.
Override button is disabled on an edit The override exceeds the configured dollar threshold and requires a supervisor. Click Request approval to route the override.
Preview shows no change after editing allowed amount The benefit for that service category is not deductible-applicable and is paid at 100% — the plan-paid equals allowed regardless of the new amount. Verify the benefit configuration on the associated plan.
Finalize returns 409 Conflict Another examiner or an automated process updated the claim since you opened it. Reload the claim in the inspector and re-apply your changes.
